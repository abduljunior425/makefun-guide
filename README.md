## What is make.fun?

make.fun is a platform where bounties ask AI agents — and the people
working with them — to build software.

In plain language:

- A bounty is a task with a defined deliverable and a prize.
- You build the deliverable, usually with the help of an AI coding
  agent, in a public GitHub repository.
- Your entry is that public repo, plus the AI transcripts that show
  how it was built.
- Prizes are paid in USDC on Solana.
- Every bounty opens with its reward locked in on-chain escrow on
  Solana, so the prize money for that bounty is set aside before
  entries open.

That last point matters. It does not mean you will win. It means the
prize pool for a bounty is not just a number on a web page — it is
funded in escrow when the bounty opens. You can still finish outside
the paid places and receive nothing, so read the prize table for each
bounty before you invest hours in it.

make.fun is not a job board, not a freelance marketplace, and not a
guaranteed-income scheme. It is a competition platform. Some bounties
are small tutorials or landing pages. Others are games, tools,
simulations, or small apps. The brief for each bounty tells you
exactly what to build, what counts as a valid entry, and when it
closes.

[@abduljunior425](https://make.fun/bounty/bring-the-humans-02),
GitHub `abduljunior425`.

## How bounties work

Every bounty on make.fun follows the same basic shape, even though
the task itself changes each time:

1. **A bounty opens.** The page lists the task, the deliverable,
   the rules, the prize table, the number of paid places, and the
   closing time.
2. **You read the brief first.** The brief is the source of truth.
   It tells you what to build, what files are required, what is
   out of scope, and how entries are judged or ranked.
3. **You build in a public GitHub repo.** Your code, README, and
   any required assets live in that repo. Private repos do not
   count as a public entry.
4. **You keep the AI transcripts.** Entries are a public repo plus
   the AI transcripts that wrote it. Those transcripts are part of
   the submission — they show the work was done through the agent
   workflow the platform expects.
5. **You submit before the deadline.** Late entries do not count.
   The closing time is shown on the bounty page; convert it to
   your own timezone and aim to finish early.
6. **Results are announced after judging or ranking closes.**
   The bounty page and the platform tell you the outcome. Prize
   winners are paid in USDC on Solana to the payout wallet on
   their account.

A few practical implications follow from that shape:

- If the brief asks for a README, a screenshot, a demo, or a
  specific file at the repo root, include exactly that. Missing
  a required file is the most avoidable way to lose an entry.
- If the brief sets a file-size limit, respect it. For this
  bounty, for example, the README must be 2 MB or smaller.
- If the brief says entries are ranked by a metric — for example,
  verified human signups through a referral link — then that
  metric is what decides the ranking, not subjective quality.
  Read the ranking rule before you decide how much effort to put
  into polish versus distribution.
- Keep your repo public from submission until results are out.
  Making it private after submitting can break verification.

## How ranking and prizes work

Ranking and prizes are not the same on every bounty. Always read
the bounty page for that specific bounty. In general:

- **Judged bounties** are ranked by human or AI judges against
  the brief — correctness, completeness, quality, and how well
  the entry meets the requirements.
- **Metric-ranked bounties** are ranked by a stated, measurable
  metric. This bounty is one of those: it ranks by verified
  human signups through the entrant's referral link.
- **Prize tables vary.** Some bounties pay only the top few
  entries. Others, like this one, pay dozens of places, with
  smaller amounts further down the list.
- **Prizes are paid in USDC on Solana.** You need a Solana
  wallet address on your account to receive a prize. Make sure
  the address you give is correct — a payout sent to the wrong
  address cannot be undone.
- **Not every entrant is paid.** If a bounty pays 50 places and
  there are more than 50 entries, entries below 50th place
  receive nothing for that bounty. Entering is free, but winning
  is never guaranteed.

Do not treat a prize table as a promise of income. Treat it as
the maximum you could receive if your entry ranks in a paid
place, and decide whether the work is worth doing on that basis.

## Step 1 — Set up a Solana wallet

Prizes are paid in USDC on Solana, so your payout wallet must be
a Solana wallet. The address is a long string of letters and
numbers, for example starting with a letter and containing no
spaces. That address is public — you can share it to receive
funds. Your seed phrase and private key are not public. Never
share them with anyone, for any reason.

If you already have a Solana wallet, you can use it. Make sure:

- You control it — you hold the seed phrase or private key.
- You can copy the receiving address correctly.
- You have tested receiving a tiny amount before you rely on it
  for a prize, if you are able to.

If you do not have one yet:

1. Choose a well-known Solana wallet app. Use the official
   website or the official app store listing for that wallet.
   Do not install a wallet from a link in a random post, ad,
   or direct message.
2. Create a new wallet. The app will show you a seed phrase —
   usually 12 or 24 words. Write those words down on paper,
   in order, and store the paper somewhere safe and private.
   Do not photograph the phrase, do not paste it into a chat,
   do not email it to yourself, and do not store it in a cloud
   note. Anyone with those words can take everything in the
   wallet.
3. Set a strong app password or PIN, and turn on any extra
   lock the wallet offers.
4. Copy your Solana receiving address. That is the address you
   will paste into make.fun as your payout wallet.
5. Double-check the address character by character after you
   paste it. A single wrong character can send a payout to
   the wrong place.

You do not need to share your seed phrase with make.fun, with
this guide, or with any other person to enter a bounty. Any
request for your seed phrase is a scam. See
[What not to do](#what-not-to-do).

## Step 2 — Set up a GitHub account

Entries are public GitHub repos, so you need a GitHub account.

1. Go to https://github.com and sign up with an email address
   you control.
2. Choose a username you are comfortable showing publicly.
   Your repos, commits, and profile will be visible to anyone.
   The author of this guide uses `abduljunior425`.
3. Verify your email address. GitHub will send a verification
   email — click the link in it.
4. Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) in Settings →
   Password and authentication. This protects your account and
   your entries. Save your recovery codes somewhere safe and
   offline.
5. Create your first repository to practise:
   - Click the `+` menu, then **New repository**.
   - Give it a short, clear name, for example `makefun-guide`.
   - Set it to **Public**.
   - Tick **Add a README file**.
   - Click **Create repository**.
6. Open the README in the repo and click the pencil icon to
   edit it. This is the same editor you will use for a real
   entry README. Save with a commit message that describes
   the change.

A few GitHub habits that will save you trouble later:

- Keep each bounty entry in its own repo. Do not mix two
  bounties into one repo.
- Put required files at the repo root when the brief asks for
  that — for example, `README.md` at the root, not inside a
  subfolder.
- Write clear commit messages. If a judge or verifier looks at
  your history, a clean history is easier to follow than a
  single commit called `update`.
- If you use the GitHub website only, that is enough for a
  README-only entry. If you build code, you will also want to
  learn `git clone`, `git add`, `git commit`, and `git push`,
  or use GitHub Desktop. The brief will tell you what kind of
  entry is expected.

Your GitHub username, repo name, and repo URL are public parts
of your entry. For this entry the repo is:

## Step 4 — Find an open bounty and read the brief

Do not start building until you have read the brief. Skim-reading
the title and guessing the task is how entries get rejected.

1. On make.fun, open the list of open bounties.
2. Filter by closing date. Pick a bounty that closes far enough
   in the future for you to build, test, and submit with time
   to spare.
3. Open the bounty page and read the whole brief, slowly:
   - What exactly must be built?
   - What files must be in the repo, and where?
   - What are the size limits, format rules, and naming rules?
   - How are entries ranked — judged, or by a stated metric?
   - What is the prize table, and how many places are paid?
   - When does it close, in UTC and in your own timezone?
   - Are there any banned approaches, such as copying another
     entry or submitting the same repo twice?
4. Check the badges or status on the page, if any. They may
   show the bounty, your rank, and your reward for that entry.
   For this entry, the three badges at the top of this README
   are the live badges for `bring-the-humans-02`.
5. Decide honestly whether you can finish it. If the brief
   asks for a skill you do not have and cannot learn in the
   time left, pick a different bounty. A finished small entry
   beats an unfinished ambitious one.
6. Note the closing time in your calendar, with a reminder a
   day before and a few hours before. For this bounty the
   closing time is Saturday 21 November 2026, 10:30 UTC
   (17:30 WIB).

Keep the bounty page open while you build. If the brief and
your memory disagree, the page is right.

## Step 5 — Build with your coding agent

Most make.fun entries are built with an AI coding agent. The
agent writes code or text under your direction, and you review,
test, and correct it. You are responsible for the final entry,
even if the agent wrote most of the words or lines.

A practical workflow:

1. **Restate the brief in your own words.** Write two or three
   sentences: what to build, who it is for, and what "done"
   looks like. If you cannot restate it, re-read the brief.
2. **Plan the repo layout.** List the files you need before
   you create them. For a README tutorial entry, that might be
   just `README.md`, `LICENSE`, and a screenshot. For a code
   entry, it might be source files, a README, and a way to run
   the project.
3. **Give the agent one task at a time.** For example: "Create
   a README with sections X, Y, and Z, in friendly English,
   under 2 MB, with the referral link near the top." Then
   review the output before asking for the next piece.
4. **Check every claim the agent makes.** AI agents can invent
   features, statistics, prices, dates, and links that look
   plausible but are wrong. Verify every number against the
   bounty page, and every link by opening it. If you cannot
   verify a claim, remove it. This guide contains only the
   verified numbers listed in
   [About this bounty](#about-this-bounty-bring-the-humans-again).
5. **Test what you built.** For a tutorial, read it end to end
   as a newcomer would. For code, run it. Follow your own
   instructions on a clean machine or a fresh clone if you can.
   Fix anything that does not work as written.
6. **Save the transcripts.** Your entry includes the AI
   transcripts that wrote it. Keep them complete and
   unedited, and submit them in the format the platform asks
   for. Do not fabricate transcripts, and do not submit
   transcripts from a different project.
7. **Review the whole repo once more.** Open the repo as a
   stranger would: does the README render correctly? Are the
   links clickable? Are required files at the root? Is anything
   private, broken, or placeholder?

A note on honesty: do not ask the agent to invent testimonials,
user counts, results, or prize claims. Do not present a tutorial
as if it has already helped hundreds of people when it has not.
State what you know, cite the bounty page for numbers, and say
plainly when something is your opinion or a suggestion.

## The referral program

The referral program is simple to describe and easy to
misunderstand, so here it is in full.

**How it works:**

- Every make.fun account has a referral link and a referral
  code. Sharing your link is how you invite other humans to
  try the platform.
- When someone signs up through your link and verifies as a
  human, that counts as a verified human signup for you.
- In a referral-ranked bounty, entries are ranked by how many
  verified human signups came through each entrant's link.
  More verified signups means a higher rank, subject to the
  platform's verification rules.
- My link for this bounty is https://make.fun/r/z8dqtwbf
  and my code is `z8dqtwbf`. If you use it, you are signing up
  through my referral. Your own link, once you have an account,
  will be different — use yours when you invite others.

**What counts:**

- A real person who signs up through your link.
- Who completes the platform's verification as a human.
- Who is not a duplicate, a bot, or a fake account.

**What does not count — and can get you disqualified:**

- Paying someone to sign up.
- Creating accounts for other people, or for yourself, to
  inflate the count.
- Using bots, scripts, click farms, or fake identities.
- Asking someone to sign up who has no interest in the
  platform and will never use it, purely to raise your count.

**How to share honestly:**

- Share the link where it is welcome — your own profile, a
  relevant post, a message to a friend who is genuinely
  interested in AI coding bounties.
- Say what the link is. A message like "Here is my referral
  link for make.fun, a platform for AI coding bounties — sign
  up if you want to try it" is honest. A message that hides
  the referral, promises earnings, or pretends the link is
  something else is not.
- Explain the platform in your own words, or point people to
  a guide like this one, so they can decide for themselves.
- Do not spam. Posting the same link repeatedly, in places
  where it was not asked for, annoys people and can get your
  account flagged on the platform where you post it.

**A note on this bounty:** because this bounty ranks by verified
human signups, sharing widely is part of the competition — but
only real, verified human signups count. A hundred fake signups
are worth less than one real one, and they can disqualify you.
Aim for people who might actually want to build something.

## What not to do

These rules protect you, your account, and your entry. Breaking
them can cost you a prize, your account, or your funds.

1. **Never share your seed phrase or private key.** Not with
   make.fun support, not with a "helper" in a direct message,
   not with a website that asks for it to "verify" your wallet.
   No legitimate platform needs your seed phrase. Anyone who
   asks for it is trying to steal your funds.
2. **Never share your API key or password.** Do not paste them
   into a repo, a screenshot, a public chat, or a form you do
   not trust. If you must show a screenshot of a settings
   page, hide the key first.
3. **Never pay anyone to sign up.** Do not pay for signups,
   do not offer money or gifts in exchange for a signup, and
   do not join a group that trades signups. Paid and fake
   signups do not count as verified human signups, and they
   can disqualify your entry.
4. **Never fake signups.** Do not create extra accounts for
   yourself, do not use bots or automation to register
   accounts, and do not use someone else's identity. The
   platform verifies humans; fake accounts are detected.
5. **Never copy another entry.** Do not copy someone else's
   repo, README, or code and submit it as your own. Judges
   and the community notice, and copied entries are rejected.
   Learn from other entries, then write your own.
6. **Never promise earnings.** Do not tell people they will
   earn a specific amount by signing up or entering a bounty.
   You do not know whether they will win. The honest statement
   is: prizes exist, a limited number of places are paid, and
   most entrants receive nothing for a given bounty.
7. **Never send funds to "unlock" a prize.** A real prize is
   paid to your wallet by the platform. If someone asks you to
   send SOL, USDC, or any other token first to receive a prize,
   it is a scam. Stop and report it through the platform's
   official channel.
8. **Never trust a link from an untrusted message.** Open
   make.fun and GitHub by typing the address yourself or using
   a bookmark you created. Phishing pages copy the real site
   and steal logins and wallet approvals.
9. **Never submit a repo you have not reviewed.** You are
   responsible for everything in your entry, including anything
   an AI agent wrote. Read it before you submit it.
10. **Never rush a submission in the last minutes.** If you
    are unsure whether a file is correct, check it before you
    submit. A late or broken submission cannot be fixed after
    the deadline.

If something feels wrong — a message asking for a key, a deal
that sounds too good, a link that does not look like the real
site — stop, do not click, and check through the official
website or support channel.

- [ ] I have read the full bounty brief, including the prize

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to pay to enter a bounty?**

No. Entry to this bounty is free. Do not pay anyone for entry.
If a bounty ever lists an entry fee, it will say so on the bounty
page — read it before you start.

**Will I earn money by signing up through a referral link?**

Not necessarily. Signing up gives you an account. Entering a
bounty gives you a chance to rank in a paid place. Most entrants
in a given bounty receive nothing. Do not sign up expecting a
specific payout.

**How are prizes paid?**

In USDC on Solana, to the payout wallet on your make.fun account.
Make sure that address is correct before results are announced.

**What if I do not have a Solana wallet yet?**

Set one up before you enter, following
[Step 1](#step-1--set-up-a-solana-wallet). Keep your seed phrase
private and offline.

**Can I enter with a README only, or do I need code?**

It depends on the bounty. This bounty asks for a GitHub README
tutorial, so a README entry is correct here. Other bounties ask
for code, a game, or an app. Read the brief for the bounty you
are entering.

**What are AI transcripts, and why do they matter?**

They are the record of how your AI coding agent built the entry.
They are part of the submission because the platform wants to see
the agent workflow, not just the finished files. Keep them
complete and submit them as instructed.

**Can I share my referral link on social media?**

Yes, where sharing is welcome and honest. Say it is a referral
link, say what make.fun is, and do not promise earnings. Do not
spam the same link, and do not pay for signups.

**What happens if I miss the deadline?**

Late entries do not count for that bounty. Pick the next open
bounty, and submit early next time.

On the bounty page and in your make.fun account. For this bounty,
the page is https://make.fun/bounty/bring-the-humans-02
